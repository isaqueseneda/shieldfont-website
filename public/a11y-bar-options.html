The reader bar

Ten layouts for the furniture that sits on a shielded block, and ten wordings for what it says, crossed with each other — pick a wording in the rail and every specimen re-renders with it.

Nothing here is mocked. The paragraphs are real alpha-encoded decoys rendered through the real shielded face, and unlocking runs the real time-lock puzzle from @shieldfont/core in a real Worker.

The original words are not in this page. Each paragraph ships as a decoy plus an AES-GCM ciphertext sealed by sealText(). The key is not here either — your browser has to grind out t sequential squarings to derive it. View source and search for any sentence you can read on screen; you will not find it. Both the reveal and the copy go through that grind: there is no path to the words that skips it.
What unlocking actually changes. Almost nothing on screen — a sighted reader could already read the block, because the font did that. What changes is that the words become selectable, copyable, translatable and readable by a screen reader. Turn on “show what a scraper reads” in the rail to see the only difference that matters.

What this file is deciding

1. Per-block furniture vs. one page-level control

Your open question from the issue. L1–L7, L9 and L10 are per-block: a ten-paragraph article gets ten rules, and ten copies of the wording ship in the HTML. L8 is the page-level answer — one bar, one string, nothing sitting on the prose — but it cannot address a single block, and a reader who only wants paragraph six has to take all ten. L10 splits it: the full explanation once, a two-icon stub on every block after.

2. How much the wording gives away

W1–W2 name the mechanism out loud. W3–W4 describe only the consequence to the reader (“this won’t copy correctly”), which tells a person everything they need and a crawler nothing worth routing around. W7 is the shortest thing that still means something. That axis is the cost you flagged: the prose is the one tell setCamouflage can’t reach. Note that W3 and W4 also read fine above unprotected text, which is your third mitigation — furniture that appears everywhere stops being evidence of anything.

3. Everything here is a real control

  • Real <button>s whose names carry the block’s position (“Show plain text for paragraph 2”), so two blocks on one page aren’t identical by ear.
  • <progress> stays in the accessibility tree, labelled, so progress can be queried on demand instead of waited for. The estimate is drawn on screen as well as announced — a progress bar with no number is a spinner.
  • One polite live region per block, silent until it has something to say. Announcement density scales with the measured wait — no interim updates under 6 s, halfway only under 20 s — which is the shipped solver’s rule, kept.
  • The revealed text is a real Tab stop and its own polite region.
  • visualHidden has no reason to exist in this design. Nothing is clipped off-screen. That answers @oddron directly: the control is drawn.
  • The encoded body keeps aria-hidden until unlocked, because a decoy read aloud is worse than silence — it’s fluent, grammatical and wrong. This is the one thing that stays hidden, and it’s the part worth arguing about.

4. Copy goes through the puzzle, not around it

Three doors, one box. Press Copy on a locked block and it grinds first, then writes. Select a locked paragraph and press ⌘C and you get the notice, never silent decoys. Unlock first and ⌘C gives you the real words with no wait, because the box is already open.

The copy button has the problem you called out: the clipboard write lands seconds after the press, and a browser may no longer count that as a user gesture. Set the puzzle to the 20 s budget and try it in Safari. When it fails the status line says so and the button stays pressable — but if it fails reliably, the honest fallback is two presses, unlock then copy, and the button has to say that up front.

5. Caching is real too

A solved block is kept in localStorage keyed on its ciphertext, so a return visit is instant — and a redeploy reseals with fresh primes and invalidates every cached solution, including the crawler’s. Reload the page and the blocks you solved come back open. Forget solved blocks in the rail clears it so you can feel the grind again.
